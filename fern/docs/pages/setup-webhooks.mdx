---
title: Configure webhooks
subtitle: Receive what happened, verified, and without applying it twice.
---

Webhooks are how your backend learns about payments, customers and wallet movement. In production they are the source of truth, not the dashboard and not what a browser or an AI client reported.

<Steps>

<Step title="Create an endpoint">

In the dashboard, under **Webhooks**, add the URL Mitu should deliver to. It must be reachable over HTTPS and answer quickly.

{/* SCREENSHOT: docs/assets/guides/quick-start/webhook-create.png
     Caption: "Adding a webhook endpoint and choosing its events." The secret
     must be masked. */}

</Step>

<Step title="Choose events">

Subscribe to what you will act on, rather than everything.

```text
Being paid      payment.completed, payment.failed
Payment links   link.created, link.viewed
Customers       customer.created
Wallets         wallet.created, wallet.credited, wallet.debited
Invoices        invoice.created, invoice.sent, invoice.paid
Subscriptions   plan.created, subscription.created, .charged,
                .past_due, .cancelled
Sessions        session.opened, session.closed
```

`payment.*` means you are being paid. `wallet.*` belongs to wallets you issue, where the money is your customer's and you are the operator. A merchant may run either or both.

<Note title="Some declared events are never sent">
`invoice.viewed`, `invoice.overdue`, `refund.completed`, `settlement.completed` and `customer.status.changed` exist as types but nothing raises them today. Subscribing to one is harmless; waiting on one is a flow that never runs.
</Note>

</Step>

<Step title="Store the secret">

Each endpoint gets its own secret.

```text
whsec_…
```

<Warning title="Not the same as your signing secret">
`whsec_…` signs deliveries Mitu sends **to you**. `mps_…` signs requests Mitu forwards to your API on an agent's behalf. They sign opposite directions, and verifying with the wrong one fails every delivery, which usually gets diagnosed as a delivery problem rather than a key problem.
</Warning>

Each endpoint's deliveries must be verified with that endpoint's own secret.

</Step>

<Step title="Verify the signature">

```http
Content-Type: application/json
X-Mitu-Signature: v1,t=1788990420,sig=<hmac-sha256>
```

Verify before you act on anything in the body. Keep the raw bytes: parsing JSON and re-encoding it changes key order and whitespace, and the comparison then fails. Use a timing-safe compare, and reject a timestamp outside a few minutes so a captured delivery cannot be replayed later.

</Step>

<Step title="Handle the envelope">

Every event has the same shape:

```json
{
  "event": "payment.completed",
  "event_id": "evt_a8f1zq5xkm2lcn6f",
  "event_created_at": "2026-09-09T14:22:57.003Z",
  "event_data": { }
}
```

Only the fields that apply are sent. On payment events, `source` tells you which shape to expect: one from a link carries no `service_id`, one from an MCP endpoint carries no `payment_link_id`.

Anything naming a customer carries both ids: `customer_id` is Mitu's, `customer_ref` is yours.

</Step>

<Step title="Deduplicate, then acknowledge">

```text
Receive
  |
Verify with this endpoint's whsec_
  |
Seen this event_id? Acknowledge and stop
  |
Apply the change
  |
Answer 2xx
```

Mitu attempts a delivery up to five times with backoff, and **every attempt carries the same `event_id`**, because a retry is the same event arriving again rather than a new one. Deduplicating on it is the only thing stopping one payment being applied twice.

Answer `2xx` to acknowledge. Anything else counts as a failure and is retried; after the last attempt the delivery is abandoned and you can resend it by hand.

</Step>

<Step title="Test it">

The Test button sends a real delivery, recorded in the log like any other, so what you test is what production sends.

Every attempt is logged with its status and response, so a delivery that failed can be inspected rather than guessed at.

</Step>

</Steps>

## What to do with the common ones

```text
payment.completed   mark your record paid, using reference
payment.failed      read reason: not all of them are worth a retry
wallet.credited     tell the customer their wallet is ready
wallet.debited      record the movement; check delivered
customer.created    store the mapping, especially from an OAuth sign-in
```

`delivered: false` on a payment or wallet event means the money moved and your endpoint did not serve the call. That is money owed rather than revenue. See [paid but not delivered](/platform/payments/agent-payments).
