---
title: Connect customer accounts
subtitle: Identify which of your customers an AI agent is acting for using a customer key or the account they already have with your product.
---

When an AI agent uses an operation that belongs to a particular customer, your product needs to know which customer the agent is acting for. Mitu supports two ways to establish that identity: customer keys and OAuth.

Customer keys work well when you want to provision access yourself. OAuth lets customers sign in with the account they already have with your product, allowing your existing authentication and authorisation rules to continue working when they use your product through an AI agent.

## Choose how customers connect

Both methods identify a Mitu customer and can be used for operations that require customer authentication. The main difference is how the customer signs in and how your backend authorises the resulting request.

| | Customer key | OAuth |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Customer experience | Connect with a key you provide | Sign in with their existing account |
| Customer credential | `mcpk_...` | Existing product login |
| Customer identity | Mitu customer record | Existing account mapped to a Mitu customer |
| Your API | Identifies the customer from Mitu's customer context | Receives the customer's access token |
| Best for | Provisioned access or products without OAuth | Products with existing customer accounts |

Use a **customer key** when you want to create the customer and provide their connection credential directly. Use **OAuth** when customers already have accounts with your product and should sign in through the same identity system they already use.

<Note title="Customer authentication is separate from Agent Wallets">
  Connecting a customer identifies who an AI agent is acting for. It does not automatically create an Agent Wallet or give the agent access to customer funds. Wallets are issued separately when you want an agent to be able to make payments on the customer's behalf.
</Note>

## Before you begin

You need a Mitu account, a connected MCP integration and at least one operation that should behave differently depending on which customer is using it.

Public operations such as product catalogues, menus or general availability may not need customer authentication at all. Account-specific operations such as order history, saved information, private resources or actions performed against a customer account usually should.

<Steps toc={true}>
  <Step title="Choose which operations require a customer">
    Decide which MCP operations need to know who the agent is acting for. Authentication is configured per operation, so public capabilities can remain available without a customer while account-specific capabilities require one.

    For example, an agent might be allowed to browse a public catalogue without signing in, while viewing saved addresses, retrieving order history or creating something against a customer account requires authentication.

    Configure those operations with customer authentication enabled.

    ```yaml
    name: create_booking
    method: POST
    path: /bookings
    requires_auth: true
    ```

    When an authenticated operation is used, Mitu requires the AI client to establish a customer identity before the request can continue.

    Learn more about [MCP authentication](/platform/mcp/authentication).
  </Step>

  <Step title="Connect customers with a customer key">
    Use customer keys when you want to provision access directly. Create or identify the customer in Mitu and provide them with the customer key returned for their account.

    ```bash
    curl --request POST \
      --url https://api.mitu.ai/v1/customers \
      --header "x-api-key: $MITU_API_KEY" \
      --header "Content-Type: application/json" \
      --data '{
        "client_reference_id": "your_user_8841",
        "first_name": "Ada",
        "last_name": "Obi",
        "email": "ada@example.com"
      }'
    ```

    A successful response identifies the customer and provides the information they need to connect.

    ```json
    {
      "id": "cus_ds9hazr8jd3l",
      "client_reference_id": "your_user_8841",
      "key": "mcpk_3a6f9d2a6bbc816b11d524fe052b26b7e75fb7b5fa7a1a7d",
      "mcp_url": "https://mcp.yourdomain.com/mcp"
    }
    ```

    Use `client_reference_id` to associate the Mitu customer with the corresponding customer in your own system. The `mcpk_...` value is the customer's connection credential, not your merchant API key, and should only be given to that customer.

    The customer adds the MCP URL to their AI client and authenticates with their customer key. Once connected, Mitu knows which customer the agent is acting for whenever it uses an operation that requires customer authentication.

    Learn more about [Customer keys](/platform/customers/customer-keys).
  </Step>

  <Step title="Connect customers with OAuth">
    Use OAuth when customers already sign in to your product and you want the same account identity to carry into the agent experience. Instead of giving the customer a separate key, configure your existing identity provider for the MCP integration.

    The customer adds your MCP URL to a compatible AI client and signs in using the account they already have with your product. Mitu uses the authenticated identity to create or associate the corresponding Mitu customer record.

    With OAuth, the customer's access token can be forwarded to your API when an authenticated operation is called. Your backend can validate that token and continue applying the same account rules, permissions and entitlements it already uses for your website or application.

    This means a customer who cannot access a resource through your existing product should not gain access to it simply because they are using an AI agent.

    Learn more about [OAuth](/platform/customers/oauth).
  </Step>

  <Step title="Keep your customer records connected">
    Whichever authentication method you use, keep the Mitu customer associated with the corresponding customer in your own system.

    With customer keys, use `client_reference_id` to provide your existing customer identifier when creating the customer. With OAuth, the authenticated identity is used to associate the customer with their existing account.

    This mapping lets your backend understand which customer an incoming agent request belongs to and lets your server perform customer-level actions through the Mitu API, such as issuing an Agent Wallet.

    Avoid creating another customer record simply because an existing customer starts using an AI agent. The agent is another way for that customer to access your product, not a new customer identity.

    Learn more about [Customers](/platform/customers/overview).
  </Step>

  <Step title="Authorise the request in your backend">
    When an authenticated operation reaches your API, use the customer identity associated with the request to apply your normal account and authorisation rules.

    With a customer key connection, Mitu identifies the customer associated with that key so your backend can resolve the request to the corresponding customer record. The `mcpk_...` credential itself is not forwarded to your API.

    With OAuth, the signed-in customer's access token is forwarded with the request so your existing authentication and authorisation logic can continue to determine what that customer is allowed to access or do.

    Customer authentication answers **who the agent is acting for**. Your application should still decide **what that customer is allowed to do** based on the rules you already enforce.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Verify requests from Mitu">
    Customer identity should only be trusted after your backend has verified that the request came through Mitu. When you connect your API, Mitu provides a signing secret that your server uses to verify forwarded requests.

    Request verification and customer authentication serve different purposes. Verification establishes that Mitu sent the request, while customer authentication establishes which customer the AI agent is acting for.

    Verify the request first, then use the customer identity or OAuth access token to apply your normal authorisation rules.

    See [Request verification](/platform/security/request-verification) for the verification process and implementation examples.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Test customer access">
    Connect a test customer through the authentication method you configured and call an operation that requires a customer. Confirm that your backend resolves the request to the correct customer and applies the same account rules you expect from your existing product.

    Test with more than one customer to make sure customer-specific data remains correctly separated. Also test an unauthenticated request to confirm that protected operations require the customer to connect before they can be used.

    If you support both customer keys and OAuth, test each flow independently because they establish the same customer context in different ways.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Customer keys and OAuth can coexist

You do not have to choose one authentication method for every customer or every integration. Customer keys can work well for customers you provision directly, while OAuth can provide a more natural experience for customers who already have accounts with your product.

Both methods ultimately establish which customer the AI agent is acting for. What changes is how that identity is established and how your existing backend authorises the resulting request.

You can also leave operations public when they do not need customer identity at all. Requiring authentication only where it is necessary keeps simple capabilities easy for agents to use while protecting account-specific actions.

## Customer identity does not change ownership

Connecting a customer to an AI agent does not transfer ownership of their account, data or funds to the agent. The agent acts on behalf of the authenticated customer and remains subject to the same permissions and account rules your product applies to that customer.

If you later issue an Agent Wallet to the customer, the wallet remains owned by the customer and wallet controls continue to apply independently of how the customer authenticated.
