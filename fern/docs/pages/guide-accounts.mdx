---
title: Connect customer accounts
subtitle: Identify which of your customers an agent is acting for, either with a key you issue or with the account they already have.
---

An agent calling your MCP is acting for somebody. Operations that touch an account need to know who. There are two ways to establish that, and they differ in who issues the credential and in what reaches your own API.

```text
Customer key   You create the customer, Mitu returns mcpk_..., you hand it over
OAuth          The customer signs in with the account they already have with you
```

Both satisfy `requires_auth` on an endpoint. Only one of them puts a token in front of your API.

## Choosing

| | Customer key | OAuth |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Who provisions | You, through the API | Nobody, the customer signs in |
| What the customer holds | `mcpk_...` | Their existing login |
| What your API receives | `x-mitu-customer-id` and `x-mitu-customer-ref` | The same, **plus** their access token as `Authorization` |
| Your existing authorisation | You authorise from the customer reference | Works unchanged |
| Use when | You provision access yourself, or run no OAuth | Customers already have accounts with you |

<Warning title="A customer key does not reach your API">
`mcpk_...` authenticates the customer **to Mitu**. It is never forwarded, and no `Authorization` header arrives with it. Your endpoint learns who is calling from `x-mitu-customer-ref` and must authorise from that.

If your API insists on a bearer token, an endpoint called with a customer key will refuse it. That is not a misconfiguration; it is the difference between the two methods. Use OAuth where your API needs the customer's own token.
</Warning>

## The four credentials

```text
sk_live_... / sk_test_...   your merchant key. Server-side. Creates customers and wallets
mcpk_...                    one customer's key for reaching your MCP. Holds no funds
mps_...                     signs requests Mitu forwards to your API
whsec_...                   signs webhook deliveries Mitu sends you
```

They are not interchangeable and only one of them ever leaves your servers.

## Part one: customer keys

<Steps toc={true}>

<Step title="Create the customer">

```bash title="Request"
curl --request POST \
  --url https://api.mitu.ai/v1/customers \
  --header "x-api-key: $MITU_API_KEY" \
  --header "Idempotency-Key: customer-your_user_8841" \
  --header "Content-Type: application/json" \
  --data '{
    "client_reference_id": "your_user_8841",
    "first_name": "Ada",
    "last_name": "Obi",
    "email": "ada@example.com",
    "phone": "+2348099887766"
  }'
```

```json title="201 Created"
{
  "id": "cus_ds9hazr8jd3l",
  "client_reference_id": "your_user_8841",
  "first_name": "Ada",
  "last_name": "Obi",
  "email": "ada@example.com",
  "phone": "+2348099887766",
  "key": "mcpk_3a6f9d2a6bbc816b11d524fe052b26b7e75fb7b5fa7a1a7d",
  "mcp_url": "https://mcp.yourdomain.com/mcp",
  "created_at": "2026-09-11T15:00:34.915Z"
}
```

<ParamField path="id" type="string">
  `cus_...`, Mitu's identifier for this customer.
</ParamField>

<ParamField path="client_reference_id" type="string">
  Your own identifier. It is what comes back on every event as `customer_ref` and on every forwarded request as `x-mitu-customer-ref`, which is how you look the person up without storing Mitu's id.
</ParamField>

<ParamField path="key" type="string">
  The customer's MCP key, **returned once**. Only a hash is stored, so it cannot be shown again.
</ParamField>

<ParamField path="mcp_url" type="string">
  Where their AI client connects.
</ParamField>

</Step>

<Step title="Store the mapping">

Keep the pairing in your own database, both ways:

```text
your_user_8841  <->  cus_ds9hazr8jd3l
```

You need `cus_...` to act on the customer from your backend, such as issuing them a wallet. You need your own reference to resolve an incoming request to a row. Events and forwarded requests carry both, so either direction works, but storing the pair saves a lookup on every call.

Do not store the key. You cannot use it, and it is the customer's credential rather than yours.

</Step>

<Step title="Handle idempotency">

Send an `Idempotency-Key` on the create.

```text
Same key, same body, within 24 hours   the original response, byte for byte
Same key, different body               409
```

A `409` means something on your side changed between attempts, not that a retry failed. The key is remembered only after the work succeeded, so a failed creation does not replay as a success.

`client_reference_id` gives you a second layer: the same reference always resolves to the same customer, so a retry without an idempotency key still will not produce a duplicate.

</Step>

<Step title="Give the customer their connection details">

Two values, and one of them is a credential:

```text
MCP URL       https://mcp.yourdomain.com/mcp
Customer key  mcpk_3a6f9d2a...
```

Deliver the key the way you would deliver any other credential. It was returned once, so capture it at creation; if a customer loses theirs, issue a new customer key rather than trying to recover it.

<Warning title="Never send a merchant key instead">
`sk_live_...` creates customers, wallets and payment links. A customer needs none of that. The only credential that should ever reach them is `mcpk_...`.
</Warning>

{/* SCREENSHOT: docs/assets/integration-guides/accounts/customer-created.png
     Caption: "A customer and their connection details." Use a clearly
     non-production key. */}

</Step>

<Step title="The customer connects">

Their client sends the key on every MCP request:

```http
x-api-key: mcpk_3a6f9d2a...
```

`Authorization: Bearer <key>` is also accepted, and a key in the URL path works too. Whichever form, Mitu resolves it to exactly one customer.

{/* SCREENSHOT: docs/assets/integration-guides/accounts/key-connection.png
     Caption: "Connecting with a customer-specific key." */}

</Step>

<Step title="Confirm identity reaches your API">

On a call to one of your endpoints, your backend receives:

```http
x-mitu-customer-id: cus_ds9hazr8jd3l
x-mitu-customer-ref: your_user_8841
x-mitu-request-id: req_5f13b916
x-mitu-signature: t=1757601046,v1=9f3c4a...
```

and **no** `Authorization` header.

Verify the signature first. The customer headers are plain headers and mean nothing until it proves the request came from Mitu; they are set after the forwarded ones precisely so a caller cannot supply their own. Then authorise from `x-mitu-customer-ref`, which maps straight to a row in your database.

</Step>

</Steps>

## Part two: OAuth

Use OAuth when your customers already sign in to your product and the agent channel should inherit that identity. Nothing is handed out, nothing is pasted into a client, and your existing account rules keep working because your own token is what reaches your API.

<Steps toc={true}>

<Step title="Turn on customer sign-in">

Enable it on your integration settings and configure your identity provider.

{/* SCREENSHOT: docs/assets/integration-guides/accounts/oauth-settings.png
     Caption: "Customer sign-in enabled, with the identity provider
     configured." No client secret visible. */}

Store the client secret in [secrets](/platform/security/keys-and-secrets) rather than pasting it into a field, so it lives in one place, rotates in one place, and never turns up in a screenshot.

</Step>

<Step title="Mark the operations that need a customer">

`requires_auth` is per endpoint. A public catalogue stays open; anything touching an account does not.

An unauthenticated call to an endpoint that requires a customer is what triggers the sign-in, so this setting is also what makes the flow start at the right moment rather than at connection time.

</Step>

<Step title="The customer adds the MCP URL">

That is all they supply. No key.

```text
https://mcp.yourdomain.com/mcp
```

</Step>

<Step title="The client discovers the flow">

An unauthenticated `initialize` answers `401` with a `WWW-Authenticate` header naming the resource metadata. That is what makes a compatible client offer to sign in rather than simply fail.

Discovery is standards-based, and Mitu publishes:

```text
/.well-known/oauth-protected-resource
/.well-known/oauth-authorization-server
/register        dynamic client registration
/authorize
/token
```

These run between the AI client and Mitu. You do not call them as part of integrating.

</Step>

<Step title="The customer signs in">

With the credentials they already use with you, against your own identity provider. Mitu never sees their password.

{/* SCREENSHOT: docs/assets/integration-guides/accounts/oauth-signin.png
     Caption: "The customer signs in with the account they already have." */}

</Step>

<Step title="Mitu creates or identifies the customer">

The first successful sign-in creates the Mitu customer from the provider's `sub`, and emits `customer.created`:

```json
{
  "event": "customer.created",
  "event_data": {
    "customer_id": "cus_0t9549fps3xd",
    "customer_ref": "auth0|68f3c2a1b9",
    "source": "oauth",
    "service_id": "svc_dhydwg80dzb4vy6xjl2m"
  }
}
```

`customer_ref` is the `sub`. Store the pairing of that `sub` and `cus_...`, because the customer id is what you pass when you later act on them from your backend.

You do not call `POST /v1/customers` first in this flow. Doing so creates a second record unless the `client_reference_id` you used already matches their `sub`.

<Note title="Signing in does not create a wallet">
It creates a customer and nothing more. [Agent Wallets](/platform/agent-wallets/overview) are a separate product, and issuing one stays an explicit action you take.
</Note>

</Step>

<Step title="The token reaches your API">

This is the part that makes OAuth different. The customer's access token is carried separately and re-emitted to you as `Authorization`:

```http
POST /bookings
authorization: Bearer <the customer's access token>
x-mitu-customer-id: cus_0t9549fps3xd
x-mitu-customer-ref: auth0|68f3c2a1b9
x-mitu-request-id: req_5f13b916
x-mitu-signature: t=1757601046,v1=9f3c4a...
```

The payment credential an agent sends on `Authorization` is always stripped before forwarding, because it is Mitu's business rather than yours. The two travel on different headers precisely so they can never be confused for one another.

</Step>

<Step title="Your existing authorisation applies unchanged">

Your endpoint validates that token exactly as it does for your own app, and applies the same entitlements, scopes and account rules. A customer who cannot see something in your product cannot see it through an agent either, and you did not have to implement that twice.

**Test it** by calling an authenticated operation as a signed-in customer and confirming it returns that customer's data, then confirming an unauthenticated call is challenged rather than served.

</Step>

</Steps>

## Anonymous operations

Neither method is required for an endpoint that does not need one. Leave `requires_auth` off and an agent can call it with no customer at all.

An agent calling anonymously belongs to nobody you have a record of, so the customer headers are absent. Treat that as ordinary, not as a fault.

## Reading customers back

```http
GET /v1/customers?limit=20
GET /v1/customers/{id}
```

Both return customers created either way. `source` on the customer distinguishes them.

## Troubleshooting

<AccordionGroup>

<Accordion title="The customer key does not work">
Check it is being sent as `x-api-key` or as a bearer, that it belongs to a customer of this service, and that it has not been replaced. Keys are shown once, so a key someone re-typed from a screenshot is a common cause. Issue a new one rather than hunting for the old.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="A customer sees another customer's data">
The key identifies exactly one customer, so this is almost always your own API authorising from something other than the customer reference, or a cached response keyed without it. Check what your endpoint uses to scope the query, and confirm it is `x-mitu-customer-ref` rather than anything the caller supplied.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="The sign-in never appears">
The client offers a sign-in because an unauthenticated call was challenged. If nothing is marked `requires_auth`, nothing challenges, and the flow never starts. Check that customer sign-in is enabled and that the endpoint being called actually requires a customer.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="OAuth succeeds but your API returns 401">
The sign-in worked and Mitu has the customer, so the problem is at your end: your endpoint is rejecting the token it received. Check that you are reading `Authorization`, that the token is from the provider you configured, and that it has not expired. A token that works in your own app and not here usually means a different audience or scope was requested.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="A customer key works for Mitu but your API returns 401">
Expected, if your endpoint requires a bearer token. A customer key never becomes one. Either authorise from `x-mitu-customer-ref`, or use OAuth for the operations that need the customer's own token.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="The OAuth customer has no wallet">
Correct. Sign-in creates a customer, not a wallet. [Issue wallets to customers](/guides/integration-guides/issue-wallets-to-customers) covers creating one for them.
</Accordion>

</AccordionGroup>

## Security

**Your merchant key** creates customers and wallets and never leaves your server. **A customer key** identifies one customer to Mitu, holds no funds, cannot create a wallet, cannot read your account, and never reaches your API. **Your OAuth client secret** belongs in secrets, not in a field. **The customer's password** never reaches Mitu: they authenticate against your provider. **Their access token** reaches your API and nowhere else.

If a customer key is lost, issue a new one. If your signing secret is rotated, the change is immediate and total, so deploy the new secret before rotating.
