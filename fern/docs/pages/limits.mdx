---
title: Limits and controls
subtitle: Configure how much an Agent Wallet can spend and when additional approval is required.
---

Spending controls let you define how an Agent Wallet should be used without giving an AI agent unrestricted access to the customer's funds. Controls are configured on individual wallets, while account-level restrictions such as a customer freeze can apply across every wallet belonging to that customer.

Wallet balances are held in USDC, while spending limits are represented in the currency specified by the limits configuration. The current wallet model supports `monthly`, `per_payment` and `approve_above`, although their enforcement behaviour is not currently the same.

## Wallet controls

| Control | Type | Current behaviour |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `monthly` | `number` | Limits how much the wallet can spend during a calendar month. Currently enforced by the payment flow. |
| `per_payment` | `number` | Configures the intended maximum for an individual payment. Stored and returned, but not currently enforced by the payment flow. |
| `approve_above` | `number` | Configures the intended threshold for customer approval. Stored and returned, but not currently enforced by the payment flow. |

<Warning title="Current enforcement">
  `monthly` is currently the spending control enforced by the payment flow. `per_payment` and `approve_above` can be configured and are returned with the wallet, but they are not currently enforced when a payment is made. Do not present those two controls to customers as enforced spending restrictions until support is available.
</Warning>

## Monthly limit

The `monthly` control defines how much a wallet can spend during a calendar month across its payments. When a payment would take the wallet beyond the configured monthly amount, Mitu does not automatically complete the payment. The payment is held for customer approval instead, allowing the person who owns the funds to decide whether that payment should proceed.

If approval is required, the agent receives a pending approval result rather than a successful payment result. The payment can continue after the customer approves it, keeping the customer involved when spending would move outside the normal wallet allowance.

## Zero and omitted controls

A control set to `0` is disabled rather than interpreted as a spending limit of zero. If you need to prevent a customer from spending entirely, use the appropriate customer-level restriction instead of relying on a zero wallet limit.

An omitted control is also different from explicitly setting a control to zero. When a wallet is created without a particular limit, that value can be inherited from the controls already configured for the customer. Your integration should therefore treat omitted, zero and positive values as distinct configurations.

## Wallet and customer controls

Wallet limits apply to the individual wallet they are configured on. A customer with separate `Travel` and `Office` wallets can therefore have different spending configurations for each balance, allowing each wallet to reflect its intended purpose.

Customer-level restrictions apply more broadly. A customer freeze or blocked destination can affect spending across the customer's wallets rather than changing only one wallet. This distinction lets you use wallet controls for individual spending pots while retaining account-level controls for restrictions that should apply to the customer as a whole.

## Example configurations

The following configurations illustrate how different wallets can carry different controls. The amounts are examples rather than recommended limits, and you should choose values appropriate for your product and customers.

```json title="Everyday spending"
{
  "per_payment": 50,
  "monthly": 500,
  "approve_above": 100
}
```

```json title="Travel"
{
  "per_payment": 500,
  "monthly": 3000,
  "approve_above": 750
}
```

Because `per_payment` and `approve_above` are not currently enforced by the payment flow, these examples describe stored wallet configurations rather than guarantees about payment behaviour. Until enforcement is available, `monthly` is the control that currently changes whether a payment can proceed without additional approval.
