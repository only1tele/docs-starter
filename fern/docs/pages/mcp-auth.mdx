subtitle: The two ways a customer proves who they are when their agent connects to your MCP.

Both methods identify the customer to Mitu. They differ in who issues the credential and where it comes from.

| Method | Use when |
| --- | --- |
| Customer key | You provision the customer and want to hand them an MCP credential |
| OAuth | Customers should sign in with the account they already have with you |

You can run either, and an endpoint's `requires_auth` field decides whether a given tool needs a signed-in customer at all.

## Customer keys

You create the customer from your own backend with your merchant key. Mitu returns an `mcpk_...` key belonging to that one customer, and you deliver it to them. They paste it into their AI client, which sends it on every MCP request:

```http
x-api-key: mcpk_3a6f9d2a...
```

`Authorization: Bearer <key>` is also accepted.

The key is returned once, at creation. Only a hash is stored, so it cannot be shown again; if a customer loses theirs, issue a new one.

<Warning title="Two different keys">
`sk_live_...` and `sk_test_...` are your server-side merchant credentials. `mcpk_...` is one customer's MCP credential. A customer key authenticates to Mitu only. It cannot create wallets, cannot read your account, and never reaches your own API. Never give a customer a merchant key.
</Warning>

[Customer keys](/platform/customers/customer-keys) covers the credential itself, and [customers](/platform/customers/customer-api) covers creating the record it belongs to.

## OAuth

Enable customer sign-in on your integration and configure your identity provider. From then on, a customer adds your MCP URL to their client and nothing else. The client discovers the authentication flow and prompts them to sign in with the credentials they already use with you.

Discovery is standards-based. Mitu publishes:

```text
/.well-known/oauth-protected-resource
/.well-known/oauth-authorization-server
/register        dynamic client registration
/authorize
/token
```

An unauthenticated `initialize` answers 401 with a `WWW-Authenticate` header naming the resource metadata, which is what prompts a client to offer a sign-in rather than fail.

These routes are part of the flow between the AI client and Mitu. You do not call them yourself as part of integrating.

After sign-in, Mitu forwards the customer's access token to your API alongside the signed request, so your existing authorisation and entitlement rules continue to apply without change.

## Choosing

Customer keys suit products where you already provision access, or where you want to hand out MCP access without touching your sign-in. OAuth suits products where the customer's identity already exists and the agent channel should inherit it, and it removes the credential-distribution problem entirely.

Neither one affects how your endpoints are written. [Bring your product to AI agents](/guides/use-cases/agent-channels) walks through setting up either from an empty account.
