---
title: Create payment experiences
subtitle: One-time payments, invoices, subscriptions and prepaid sessions, each payable by a person or an AI agent.
---

Choose the experience that matches how you charge. Use a one-time payment for a single purchase, an invoice when you need to bill a customer and track it, a subscription for recurring charges, or a session when the payer should fund a balance and draw it down.

Whichever you choose, Mitu can give the payment two ways in: a checkout page a person opens, and a payment URL an agent calls. The obligation, your reference and your reconciliation stay the same either way.

## What you can create

| You want to | Use |
| --- | --- |
| Collect one payment | [A one-time payment](#one-time-payments) |
| Bill a customer and track the lifecycle | [An invoice](#invoices) |
| Charge on a recurring basis | [A subscription](#subscriptions) |
| Let a payer fund a balance and draw it down | [A session](#prepaid-sessions) |
| Charge against calls to a path | [Per-request](#per-request) |

These are separate API resources, not one object wearing different hats. An invoice has line items and a lifecycle; a subscription has a plan and recurs; a payment link is a charge with two entry points. Pick by the job.

## Before you begin

You need a Mitu merchant account, a server-side API key, something that needs paying for, a stable reference from your own system, and a webhook endpoint for production.

Keys are issued as `sk_live_...` or `sk_test_...`. Either way they stay on your server: nothing a payer touches needs one.

## One-time payments

The simplest case. You know the amount, and either a person or an agent will pay it.

<Steps toc={true}>

<Step title="Create the payment">

```bash title="Request"
curl --request POST \
  --url https://api.mitu.ai/v1/payment-links \
  --header "x-api-key: $MITU_API_KEY" \
  --header "Content-Type: application/json" \
  --data '{
    "type": "charge",
    "amount": 25.00,
    "currency": "USD",
    "description": "Invoice 8841",
    "reference": "ORDER-8841",
    "expiresAt": "2026-10-01T00:00:00Z",
    "paymentMethods": ["x402", "mpp_spt"],
    "metadata": { "order_id": "8841" }
  }'
```

<ParamField path="type" type="charge | subscription | session | per_request" default="charge">
  Which lifecycle this payment has.
</ParamField>

<ParamField path="amount" type="number" required={true}>
  Required for `charge` and `per_request`. A `session` uses `sessionMaxDeposit`; a `subscription` takes its amount from the plan.
</ParamField>

<ParamField path="currency" type="string" required={true}>
  Required except on a `subscription`, where it comes from the plan.
</ParamField>

<ParamField path="description" type="string">
  Shown to the payer at checkout.
</ParamField>

<ParamField path="reference" type="string">
  Your own identifier. It comes back on the payment event and is what makes reconciliation possible without storing Mitu's id. Set it.
</ParamField>

<ParamField path="expiresAt" type="string">
  ISO 8601. After it the payment stops being payable.
</ParamField>

<ParamField path="paymentMethods" type="array">
  Any of `x402`, `mpp_crypto`, `mpp_spt`. Constrains what an agent may settle with. Offering more than one costs nothing and widens who can pay.
</ParamField>

<ParamField path="metadata" type="object">
  Free-form values returned intact. Not a place for secrets.
</ParamField>

</Step>

<Step title="Take both entry points">

The response carries `checkoutUrl`. Both URLs arrive together on `link.created`:

```json title="link.created"
{
  "payment_link_id": "pay_n8d7piu1yc6h",
  "type": "charge",
  "amount": "25.00",
  "currency": "USD",
  "reference": "ORDER-8841",
  "url": {
    "checkout": "https://mitu.ai/pay/pay_n8d7piu1yc6h",
    "agent": "https://api.mitu.ai/pay/pay_n8d7piu1yc6h"
  }
}
```

<Warning title="They are not interchangeable">
The checkout URL is a page served from the web app. The agent URL answers a payment requirement from the API. Send an agent the checkout URL and it gets HTML where it expected a challenge; open the agent URL in a browser and there is no page to pay on.
</Warning>

</Step>

<Step title="Let a person pay">

They open the checkout URL, see who is charging them, what for, how much, and the methods available, and pay. `link.viewed` fires when the page is opened.

{/* SCREENSHOT: docs/assets/integration-guides/payments/human-checkout.png
     Caption: "A person opens the checkout URL and pays." Use the real
     checkout. */}

</Step>

<Step title="Let an agent pay">

```text
GET  https://api.mitu.ai/pay/pay_n8d7piu1yc6h   answers 402 with the challenges
POST https://api.mitu.ai/pay/pay_n8d7piu1yc6h   takes the signed credential
```

The 402 states the amount and lists the methods you accepted. It is not a decline: it is the protocol saying what must happen for the payment to continue.

You do not implement anything for this. Mitu handles the settlement underneath. [Payment methods](/platform/payments/agent-payments) covers what an agent can pay with, if you want the detail.

</Step>

<Step title="Reconcile">

```text
payment.completed  ->  reference: ORDER-8841  ->  your row  ->  mark it paid
```

Your handler does not branch on who paid. Read `reference`, find the record, mark it.

Do not mark anything paid from the payer's browser or from what an AI client reported. Use the verified webhook, or read the state back from the API.

</Step>

<Step title="Read and update">

```text
GET    /v1/payment-links       list, filtered by type, status, reference or source
GET    /v1/payment-links/{id}  read one
PATCH  /v1/payment-links/{id}  amend one
DELETE /v1/payment-links/{id}/cancel
```

Filtering the list by your own `reference` answers "is there already a payment for this order" without you having stored Mitu's id.

`PATCH` changes `expiresAt`, `description`, `reference` and `metadata`. It does **not** change the amount or currency: to charge a different figure, cancel and create another, so a payment somebody is already looking at cannot change price under them.

</Step>

</Steps>

### The lifecycle

```text
pending -> active -> paid -> settled
                  \-> expired
                  \-> cancelled
```

`partial` and `settling` also appear, for payments part-funded or mid-settlement.

## Invoices

Use an invoice when a payment link would be too thin: line items, tax, a discount, a due date, an invoice number, and a record that moves through states rather than simply being paid.

```bash title="Create"
curl --request POST \
  --url https://api.mitu.ai/v1/invoices \
  --header "x-api-key: $MITU_API_KEY" \
  --header "Content-Type: application/json" \
  --data '{
    "customerId": "cus_ds9hazr8jd3l",
    "invoiceNumber": "INV-2026-441",
    "description": "Design services, September",
    "currency": "USD",
    "lineItems": [
      { "name": "Design retainer", "quantity": 1, "amount": 1200.00 },
      { "name": "Additional revisions", "description": "3 rounds",
        "quantity": 3, "amount": 150.00 }
    ],
    "taxAmount": 123.75,
    "discountAmount": 0,
    "dueAt": "2026-10-01T00:00:00Z"
  }'
```

An invoice is addressed to a customer, so `customerId` is required. Each line carries `name`, `quantity`, `amount` and an optional `description`.

```text
POST   /v1/invoices             create a draft
POST   /v1/invoices/{id}/send   send it to the customer
GET    /v1/invoices             list
GET    /v1/invoices/{id}        read one
PATCH  /v1/invoices/{id}        amend
DELETE /v1/invoices/{id}/cancel cancel
```

Creating and sending are separate calls, so a draft can be checked or corrected before the customer ever sees it.

A sent invoice is payable the same two ways, on the `/invoice/` segment:

```text
https://mitu.ai/invoice/inv_5v6nxq2zlk9mpd8t
https://api.mitu.ai/invoice/inv_5v6nxq2zlk9mpd8t
```

`invoice.created`, `invoice.sent` and `invoice.paid` are emitted. `payment.completed` also fires when the money arrives, carrying `source: "invoice"`.

<Note title="Two declared invoice events are not emitted">
`invoice.viewed` and `invoice.overdue` exist as event types but nothing raises them today. Do not build dunning on `invoice.overdue`: derive overdue from `dueAt` and the paid state.
</Note>

## Subscriptions

A plan describes what recurs. A subscription attaches a payer to one.

```bash title="Create a plan"
curl --request POST \
  --url https://api.mitu.ai/v1/subscription-plans \
  --header "x-api-key: $MITU_API_KEY" \
  --data '{
    "name": "Pro",
    "description": "Unlimited lookups and priority support",
    "amount": 49.00,
    "currency": "USD",
    "billingInterval": "month",
    "features": ["Unlimited lookups", "Priority support"]
  }'
```

```bash title="Subscribe someone"
curl --request POST \
  --url https://api.mitu.ai/v1/subscriptions \
  --header "x-api-key: $MITU_API_KEY" \
  --data '{
    "subscriptionPlanId": "plan_7h2kq9xz",
    "paymentMethod": "mpp_crypto",
    "subscriberId": "cus_ds9hazr8jd3l",
    "subscriberType": "user"
  }'
```

`billingInterval` is `day`, `week`, `month` or `year`. A subscription is `active`, `past_due`, `paused`, `cancelled` or `expired`, and each charge produces an invoice of its own.

<Warning title="Recurring charges settle over MPP only">
`paymentMethod` takes `mpp_crypto` or `mpp_spt`. An x402 authorisation is signed for one specific amount and recipient, so it cannot carry a future charge, and passing `x402` is rejected.
</Warning>

```text
POST /v1/subscriptions/{id}/cancel    stop it
GET  /v1/subscriptions/{id}/invoices  what it has generated
```

To let a payer choose, create a payment link of type `subscription` carrying a `planId`, or several through `planIds` with `allowCustomerChoice`. Amount and currency come from the plan rather than the link.

`plan.created`, `subscription.created`, `subscription.charged`, `subscription.past_due` and `subscription.cancelled` are all emitted.

## Prepaid sessions

A payment link of type `session` has the payer fund a balance up front and draw it down across many calls, rather than settling each one.

```json
{
  "type": "session",
  "currency": "USD",
  "sessionMaxDeposit": 50.00,
  "sessionExpiresAt": "2026-10-01T00:00:00Z",
  "reference": "SESSION-8841"
}
```

`sessionMaxDeposit` replaces `amount` here, and is the ceiling on what may be locked.

```text
session.opened   the deposit, the max, the on-chain channel and the tx hash
session.closed   what was spent, what was refunded, and its own tx hash
```

Use it where a payment per call would dominate the exchange: long or chatty interactions, metered work, anything where the payer would rather commit once.

## Per request

A link of type `per_request` prices calls against a path pattern, set with `endpointPattern`.

If what you want is to charge for calls to your API, [selling your API to agents](/guides/use-cases/api-monetisation) is the fuller treatment and [accept payments from agents](/guides/integration-guides/accept-payment-from-agents) the implementation. A `per_request` link is the standalone version of the same idea.

## Human and agent payers

You do not implement two payment systems.

```text
A person   opens the checkout and pays with what is available to them
An agent   calls the payment URL, reads the 402, and settles it
```

Both produce `payment.completed` carrying your `reference`. The protocols underneath differ; your reconciliation does not.

<Note title="The agent protocols are not your problem">
x402 and MPP are how Mitu settles an agent's payment. You accept them by listing them in `paymentMethods`, and nothing in your backend has to know how either works.
</Note>

## Webhooks

Production payment state comes from webhooks, not from a browser, an AI client or the dashboard.

```json title="Every event shares this envelope"
{
  "event": "payment.completed",
  "event_id": "evt_a8f1zq5xkm2lcn6f",
  "event_created_at": "2026-09-09T14:22:57.003Z",
  "event_data": { }
}
```

```http
Content-Type: application/json
X-Mitu-Signature: v1,t=1788990420,sig=<hmac-sha256>
```

<Warning title="whsec_ and mps_ sign opposite directions">
`whsec_...` signs webhook deliveries Mitu sends you, and is the one that matters here. `mps_...` signs requests Mitu forwards to your API on an agent's behalf. Verifying a webhook with the wrong secret fails every delivery, and usually gets diagnosed as a delivery problem rather than a key problem.
</Warning>

Mitu attempts each delivery up to five times with backoff. **Every attempt carries the same `event_id`**, because a retry is the same event arriving again rather than a new one, so deduplicating on it is what stops one payment being applied twice. Answer `2xx` to acknowledge; anything else is retried, and after the last attempt the delivery is abandoned and can be resent by hand.

The Test button sends a real delivery, recorded in the log like any other, so what you test is what production sends.

## Reconciliation

```text
Set reference at creation
        |
payment.completed arrives carrying it
        |
Verify the signature, check event_id
        |
Find your own record, mark it paid
```

Set `reference` to something your system already knows: `ORDER-8841`, `INV-2026-441`, `BOOKING-9832`. Without it, every reconciliation has to start from a Mitu id you had to store and keep in step.

### Payment state is not fulfilment state

Being paid and having delivered are two different questions, and `payment.completed` answers only the first. On a paid API call it carries `delivered`, which answers the second. Keep them as separate fields in your own records: a payment that settled against work you did not do is money owed, not revenue. See [paid but not delivered](/platform/payments/agent-payments).

## Failures

`payment.failed` fires when a payment was attempted and did not complete, with a `reason`:

```text
no_supported_method    the payer could satisfy none of the methods you accept
insufficient_balance   they did not have the funds
over_limit             a wallet limit stopped it
settlement_failed      the settlement itself did not go through
upstream_rejected      refused upstream
```

They call for different responses. `no_supported_method` usually means the methods on the payment are too narrow for the payers reaching you; `settlement_failed` is worth retrying.

## Settlement

Everything you receive lands in your Mitu balance as USDC, whoever paid and however. From there you settle in local currency to your bank account, or withdraw the stablecoin on-chain.

How somebody paid has no bearing on how you take the money out. Payouts are a dashboard action; there is no payout endpoint in the public API, because a key that could move your balance to an arbitrary address would be a key worth stealing.

<Note title="settlement.completed is not emitted">
The event type exists but nothing raises it. Reconcile against `payment.completed`.
</Note>

[Payouts](/platform/payments/settlement) covers both routes.

## Worked examples

<Steps>

<Step title="A one-time order">
Create a `charge` for the order total with `reference: ORDER-8841`. Send the checkout URL in the confirmation email. When `payment.completed` arrives, find `ORDER-8841` and release the order.
</Step>

<Step title="An invoice">
Create the invoice against the customer with line items and a due date, then send it. The customer pays at the checkout URL, or forwards it to an assistant that pays the agent URL. `invoice.paid` and `payment.completed` both arrive; you mark the invoice settled against your own number.
</Step>

<Step title="A subscription">
Create a `Pro` plan at \$49 a month. Subscribe the customer with `mpp_crypto`. Each cycle produces an invoice and a `subscription.charged` event; a failure produces `subscription.past_due`, which is your cue to tell them.
</Step>

</Steps>
